## Findings by topic

### 1. Israeli Protection of Privacy Law (חוק הגנת הפרטיות) and Amendment 13

**1.1 Registration, notification, privacy officer: probably none required.**
Amendment 13 took effect on 14 August 2025. Registration of a database is now required only for public bodies and for data brokers (databases of more than 10,000 people whose purpose is passing information to others). A notification to the Privacy Protection Authority is required for databases with "special sensitivity" information on more than 100,000 people. A privacy protection officer (ממונה) is required for public bodies, data brokers, and organizations whose *main activity* is large-scale processing of especially sensitive data or systematic monitoring of people. GiveMyTime is none of these: a private individual, a few thousand people at most, not selling data, and sensitive data only incidentally, in the raw chat. **So: no registration, no notification, no officer.** You can tell the lawyer that you'd like this confirmed, especially because the raw chat contains health information (another reason for M2).
Sources: [Gornitzky: Amendment 13 summary](https://www.gornitzky.com/an-amendment-to-the-israeli-protection-of-privacy-law-will-increase-exposure-for-violation-of-data-protection-requirements/) · [Pearl Cohen: amendment takes effect 14 Aug 2025](https://www.pearlcohen.com/major-amendment-to-israeli-privacy-law-set-to-take-effect/) · [gov.il: duty to notify the Authority about a database](https://www.gov.il/he/service/notice-obligation) · [Arnon Tadmor-Levy](https://arnontl.com/news/major-update-to-israeli-privacy-law-enacted-as-law/) · [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/israel-marks-a-new-era-in-privacy-law-amendment-13-ushers-in-sweeping-reform)

**1.2 Notice and consent: this is what applies to you.**
- **Users (Google sign-in, reviews, requests, contact relay):** §11 requires a notice when you collect personal data from someone: what for, who receives it, and whether they have to provide it. Amendment 13 made failures here enforceable by the Authority and added grounds for damages without proof of harm. The new privacy policy plus the existing "by signing in you agree" line in `ConnectionGate.tsx` covers this, as long as the policy is accurate (M7).
- **WhatsApp group members:** their data wasn't collected from them by the app. The relevant rules are §2(9) of the law (using information about someone's private affairs for a purpose other than the one it was given for counts as an invasion of privacy) and the general rules on purpose and minimization. Publishing reworded, anonymous recommendations about *businesses* is low risk. Keeping their phone numbers next to what they wrote, and leaking them publicly, is the real exposure (M1, M2). Giving notice and an opt-out (M5 and the group notice) is the reasonable remedy. Consent from everyone isn't practical, and a lawyer may advise that it isn't needed for anonymized content. **Lawyer question.**
- **Listed providers (doctors, caregivers, owners):** names and work contact details of professionals in their professional role are ordinary business information. Private caregivers are different (M4).
- **Damages:** courts can award statutory damages without proof of harm for privacy violations (amounts vary by ground; some sources cite up to ₪100,000 after Amendment 13, so verify with the lawyer). [Mineos summary](https://mineos.ai/hub-articles/israels-amendment-13-a-new-era-for-privacy) · [law text (Wikisource)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7_%D7%94%D7%92%D7%A0%D7%AA_%D7%94%D7%A4%D7%A8%D7%98%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA)

**1.3 Data Security Regulations 2017: which level.**
The regulations have four levels. The lightest, "managed by an individual" (up to 3 people with access), doesn't apply when the database's purposes include *making information available to others*, and publishing information about named providers arguably is exactly that. So expect the **basic** level. If you keep "special categories" (health and similar, which the raw chat contains), the database moves up to **medium**, with audits, access logs and breach notification. **Deleting the raw chat and raw quotes (M2) is the cheapest way to stay at basic.** In practice, basic means: a database definition document, a short security procedure, access control, logging security incidents, and secure communication (S5). Your providers (Supabase, Vercel) handle the infrastructure side.
Source: [law.co.il: overview of the 2017 Data Security Regulations](https://www.law.co.il/en/articles/2017/03/26/dramatic-overhaul-israeli-data-security-regulations/)

**1.4 Transfer abroad.**
Everything is abroad: Vercel (US), Supabase (Singapore, ap-southeast-1), OpenAI (US), Anthropic/Claude (US; the chat was read in Claude sessions), Resend (US), Mapbox (US), Google (US). The 2001 Transfer Abroad Regulations allow a transfer under one of several alternative conditions, including the data subject's consent and an agreement in which the recipient undertakes to meet Israeli requirements "with the necessary changes". The Authority's April 2026 position paper reads that agreement broadly: limited use, data subject rights, and security commitments or ISO 27001. **Practical step:** accept each provider's DPA (most of them reference ISO 27001 or SOC 2), and disclose the countries in the policy, which also supports the consent route. For a free community site this is reasonable; the lawyer can confirm. Sources: [Gornitzky on the April 2026 PPA position paper](https://www.gornitzky.com/cross-border-data-transfer-agreements-the-position-of-the-israeli-privacy-protection-authority-2/) · [Barnea Jaffa Lande](https://barlaw.co.il/practice_areas/high-tech/cyber/client_updates/transfer-of-personal-data-abroad-israeli-ppa-sharpens-requirements-for-organizations/) · [Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/israeli-privacy-regulator-clarifies-requirements-for-cross-border-data-transfers/)

**1.5 AI processing.** The chat was sent to OpenAI (`gpt-4o-mini`, in `scripts/places_import/parse_whatsapp.py` and `src/lib/whatsapp-summarize.ts`) with sender names and phones in the prompt, and it was read in Claude sessions. API data from both is not used for training by default and is kept for a limited time, but it is still a transfer and a disclosure. The policy draft discloses it. Going forward, strip sender names and phones from the text before sending it to any AI (replace them with `[A]`, `[B]`).

### 2. Republishing the WhatsApp recommendations

- **Consent:** nobody in the group agreed to their recommendations appearing on a website. What lowers the risk: the content is about businesses, it's reworded, it's anonymous, and it's useful to the same community. What raises it: the group is private, the export holds 56k messages, phone numbers were stored and leaked (M1), and there's no notice yet. **Reasonable remedy:** fix M1 and M2, publish only reworded positive or neutral recommendations about businesses, post the notice (with the admin's blessing), honour every removal request, and never import messages from someone who opted out (keep a do-not-import list of keyed hashes, not phone numbers).
- **Anonymization quality:** the prompt forbids identifying details, and `PHONE_REGEX` blocks phone numbers in summaries (`decision/route.ts`). What's left to fix: (a) a group member can find the original by searching the chat, which you can't fix, so disclose it; (b) dates (S9); (c) details about the child ("לבן שלי עם ADHD"), which the prompt should explicitly exclude (S9); (d) rare places with a single recommender, where the context alone may point to the writer. During triage, skip a summary that contains a personal story.
- **Group expectations:** groups like this tend to have rules against commercial use. Ask the admin and frame it as a community project (it is free). A notice from you, rather than people finding out, makes a big difference.
- **WhatsApp's own terms** govern your account, not the content. Exporting a chat is a built-in feature. This isn't the main risk.

### 3. Reviews about identifiable people and businesses: defamation (לשון הרע)

**3.1 Exposure.** The Defamation Prohibition Law, 1965, covers publications that can degrade a person *or harm them in their business or profession* (§1). A business, a doctor and a gan are all covered. Each person who publishes is responsible (§11). Defences: truth plus public interest (§14), and good faith in one of the listed circumstances (§15), which in practice covers honest opinion based on true facts. **§7A allows up to ₪50,000 (indexed) per publication without proof of damage, double with intent.** Sources: [law text (Wikisource)](https://he.wikisource.org/wiki/%D7%97%D7%95%D7%A7_%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A1%D7%95%D7%A8_%D7%9C%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F_%D7%94%D7%A8%D7%A2) · [Hamichlol on §7A and the defences](https://www.hamichlol.org.il/%D7%AA%D7%91%D7%99%D7%A2%D7%AA_%D7%9C%D7%A9%D7%95%D7%9F_%D7%94%D7%A8%D7%A2)

**3.2 You as the publisher.** For user reviews posted directly, Israeli courts have generally not treated site operators as publishers of user content by default, and they look at control and response. The "talkback bill" that would have made sites liable was frozen ([JPost](https://www.jpost.com/Israel/Panel-freezes-Web-site-talkbacks-law)), and the Supreme Court held that a "like" isn't publication while a share can be ([Ebn Law](https://ebnlaw.co.il/precedent-judgment-liability-of-users-on-social-networks-under-the-defamation-law)). **The WhatsApp import is different: you chose the content, an AI rewrote it on your instructions, and it appears under your site's label.** That is close to being the author. Hence M3.

**3.3 Takedown.** There's no statutory notice-and-takedown in Israel, but acting quickly once told is what courts reward in practice. Process (M6 and the terms): (1) report link → (2) hide the review within 48 hours if the complaint is plausible (a named person, a factual allegation, a safety or abuse claim) → (3) decide within 7 days: restore, edit or delete → (4) log it. **Right to respond:** let a business ask for a correction of its details, add a short response under a review (later; for now, by email), or ask to be removed. **Removal for private individuals (caregivers, babysitters): immediate, no questions asked.**

**3.4 Prompt addition for S9** (add to the summarizer prompt in `src/lib/whatsapp-summarize.ts`, after the anonymity bullet):
> - נסח כחוויה ודעה של הממליצים ("לפי ההמלצה…", "היו מרוצים מ…"), לא כעובדה מוחלטת.

### 5. Terms, disclaimers, age, accessibility, cookies

**5.1 Terms of use:** drafted (`terms.he.md`). It covers content rules (opinion, not allegations; no private individuals without consent; no health information about others), the report and removal process, "information may be inaccurate", "not medical or professional advice", the WhatsApp-derived content, and 18+ to post.

**5.2 Disclaimers** limit expectations. They can't override your legal duties. Keep them reasonable (unfair clauses in standard-form terms aren't enforceable).

**5.3 Accessibility.** Under the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law and regulation 35 of the 2013 Service Accessibility Regulations (as amended in 2017), websites that provide a service to the public must meet IS 5568 (≈ WCAG 2.0 AA). There are exemptions by turnover: per Kol Zchut, an automatic exemption up to ₪1,000,000 annual turnover, and an exemption for sites with up to 500 registered users. The exemption is conditioned on publishing contact details accessibly. A free site run by a private individual with zero turnover is very likely exempt, or not a "service provider" at all. **Recommendation:** publish the short statement (N1) with a contact for accessibility problems. It's cheap, honest, and blocks the demand letters that target sites with no statement. [Kol Zchut: exemption from website accessibility](https://www.kolzchut.org.il/he/%D7%A4%D7%98%D7%95%D7%A8_%D7%9E%D7%97%D7%95%D7%91%D7%AA_%D7%94%D7%A0%D7%92%D7%A9%D7%94_%D7%9C%D7%90%D7%AA%D7%A8%D7%99_%D7%90%D7%99%D7%A0%D7%98%D7%A8%D7%A0%D7%98_%D7%95%D7%90%D7%A4%D7%9C%D7%99%D7%A7%D7%A6%D7%99%D7%95%D7%AA) · [Chamber of Commerce on the 2017 amendment](https://www.chamber.org.il/serviceslobby/legal/1859/2151/)

**5.4 Cookies and localStorage.** Israel has no cookie-consent law like the EU's. The app uses no ad trackers and no analytics scripts (none in `package.json`). `localStorage` holds the Supabase session, saved places (`gmt_saved`, `gmt_saved_progress`), the chosen category, intro/skip-login flags and custom categories. Mapbox GL also sends its standard map-load usage events. Disclosing this in the policy is enough; no banner needed.

### 6. Email: Resend relay and the anti-spam law (§30A of the Communications Law)

§30A forbids sending a "דבר פרסומת" (a commercial message encouraging a purchase or spending) without prior consent, with statutory damages of up to ₪1,000 per message. The contact relay is a person-to-person message one user asked to send, not advertising. Transactional and admin emails (a reply to a removal request, a note that a review was approved) aren't advertising either. **Risks to manage:** (a) the relay could be abused to send spam *through* you, so add a rate limit and use the verified sender email (S7); (b) never use the collected emails for promotion, newsletters or donation appeals without a separate opt-in (N4). Make sure Resend's sending domain has SPF and DKIM (already done for givemytimeapp.com per your notes). Sources: [Chamber of Commerce: spam law refresher](https://www.chamber.org.il/serviceslobby/legal/74023/115485/) · [law.co.il rulings on "דבר פרסומת"](https://www.law.co.il/media/computer-law/avraham5.pdf)
